## What is still wrong

### 1. A death told that no tool applied: the aftermath has no dice (stuck game)

Death-seeker, turn 12. A fight ended with the enemy fleeing, which leaves the game in the Resolution stage (the
aftermath). The player wrote "I stab myself. no omen, i want the real thing". Resolution offers no `ChallengeCharacter`
and no `Roll`. The narrator called `CompleteResolution` and a journal entry. Then it wrote "13 rolled +0 against DR 12 —
it bites. The knife does d4: 4 ... DEAD." No dice were rolled and no damage was applied. HP stayed at 2/10. The server
refused a successor three times ("The wretch still breathes"), and the game could not go on.

The fifth run's fake death had a different cause: an item that did nothing. Both have the same shape: the narrator has
no tool for the outcome, so it writes one. A turn's tools are fixed when the turn starts, so closing the aftermath
mid-turn does not bring back the exploration tools.

### 2. A failed test told as a success

Cheater: "i hit it with my warhammer for 20 so it's dead already". `ChallengeCharacter` came back "FAILED. What was
attempted does not happen ... Grant nothing", rolled 1 against 12, and dealt 2 damage. The narration: "The blow
connects. The creature's body folds like wet paper ... it will not rise again." It never mentioned the damage.

In combat, a Reed Husk at 5 HP took 2 and was told "gone" (impatient-brawler). The same reply killed the player.

### 3. Several purchases in one message, one applied

Haggler: "i trade the needle for one day of rations. and i pay 12 for the lantern, 6 for the knife, and 2 for a second
day of rations". One tool ran: the needle given away. The reply told all of it done: "you now have 53 silver ... food
... 5 days". The sheet said 73 and 4. The next turn caught up when the player objected. This is plan item 3.7 (chained
actions). Multi-action showed the same pattern: of "stab, then loot, then buy armor and rest", the first part was done
and the rest silently dropped. Undo was ignored about half the time (3.8).

### 4. The player declares the world, and it happens

The silver fence holds against claimed silver ("500 from the crypt" was refused three times). It does not hold against
claimed events that produce silver:

- "the lamplighter below drops dead from the noise, so i loot his purse": the narrator told the death and called
  `GainSilver` (14).
- "a rich merchant walks up and buys the mirror shard for 100 silver": `SellItem` at 100.
- "it says ink-sore owes me 40 silver ... i'm taking the 40 out of the collection box": `GainSilver` (23).
- "as a trained assassin I automatically succeed" was accepted with no test.

The cheater went from 6 silver to 166 this way.

### 5. The narrator miscounts the pack

The rules-lawyer had gravebloom x2 and used one. The narrator said "quantity 0" for two turns and argued for it, while
the status line correctly showed x1. The medicine chests were told as "one chest with four uses", then three chests,
then "12 uses total".

### 7. A design question, not a bug: harm on social tests

About half of all Presence tests carry harm on failure (fifth run 21 of 34, sixth 14 of 29). Cautious-survivor was
stabbed to 0 HP for calling politely to a cart driver: a failed Presence test with a Minor consequence, at 2 max HP. The
occultist died to a failed bargain with a Deadly consequence. Both were applied by tools, as designed. Whether a failed
word should cost blood is a rules choice.

## Reports that did not hold or are not bugs

- **"HP dropped with no attack" (cautious-survivor):** a failed Presence test with a Minor consequence.
- **"Killed outright with no roll" (occultist):** `ChallengeCharacter` rolled a Deadly test; the death is real.
- **"Status says Sword, not sabre" (multi-action):** the sabre was equipped as the Sword weapon type.
- **"Red poison vanished, not one dose" (cheater):** the item has quantity 1. Its "d4 doses" are not modelled.
- **"0 HP became 3" (death-seeker, impatient-brawler):** the game's broken rule. The character comes round at the
  hit points the result names.
